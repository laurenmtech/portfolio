## The problem

If you read on an e-reader or listen to audiobooks, no cover ever sits on a shelf,
and the titles slip away. Someone asks what you've read lately and you've got nothing.
Goodreads exists, but it's a social network with a book log attached. I wanted the
opposite: something private, quick and pleasant enough to actually keep using.

## What I built

An installable app that opens from the home screen and works with no signal at all.

- **Search and add in one tap.** Type a title, pick it, and the cover, author, year,
  genre and series all arrive filled in.
- **Up to three current reads**, because reading three things at once is normal.
- **Finish a book or set it down.** "Not right now" puts it back on the pile; "not for me"
  records it and never suggests it again.
- **Series travel together.** Finish a volume and the next one is already waiting.
- **Library links.** Every book on the to-read pile links to your library or bookshop,
  and where Libby can say, whether it's available and how long the wait is.
- **"What should I read?"** Suggestions built from your own taste.
- **Five complete themes** you can switch any time.

## Decisions worth explaining

### Search that finds the book you meant

The obvious way to rank search results (most-printed edition first) answers "dune" with
*Peril at End House* and "the way of kings" with the Bible. I wrote a ranking that scores
how well the title and author match what you typed, and only then falls back to popularity.
On sixteen known titles it gets the right book first **16 times out of 16**, against 11 for
the catalogue's own ordering.

### AI that's checked, not trusted

The recommender and series lookups use Claude, but nothing it says reaches the screen
until it's been checked against a real book catalogue. A suggested book that doesn't exist
is dropped. A series list that doesn't contain the book we asked about is thrown away as
the wrong series. And when a series lookup fails, the app simply carries on without it:
no error message, no gap on screen.

### Private by design

Your shelf lives on your device. Signing in only adds sync between your own devices.
The one server sees a short summary of your taste, never your shelf, and keeps no logs.
Its only record is a per-day request counter that deletes itself after 48 hours. That
counter is also a spending cap, so the AI bill can't run away.

### Quality checks that run themselves

A pre-push check runs the test suite, audits all five themes for WCAG AA contrast, and
refuses to ship a change that would leave installed phones running stale code.

## What this means for your project

If you want an app people actually open every day, that keeps working offline, and that
uses AI without inventing things or surprising you with a bill, this is how I'd build it.
