## The problem

Popular food trackers bury the basics under ads, upsells and account walls, and they
send everything you eat to someone else's server. I wanted a fast tracker I'd use
every day: one tap from my home screen, logging a meal in seconds, with nothing to sign up for.

## What I built

An installable app that tracks food and workouts, with all of the data kept on the phone.

- **Food diary** with breakfast, lunch, dinner and snacks, calories remaining, and protein,
  carbs and fat against your goals. Swipe between days, or copy yesterday's meal in one tap.
- **7,793 foods built in.** The USDA's generic food database is bundled into the app, so
  search works with no signal. Brand and restaurant items come from the USDA's live API.
- **Barcode scanning** that uses the phone's built-in scanner on Android and a bundled
  fallback on iPhone, with two databases behind it.
- **Recipes** built from weighed ingredients, then logged by the gram or by the piece.
- **Share a food or recipe as a link.** The food is packed into the link itself, so nothing
  is uploaded anywhere.
- **Workout logger** with an exercise library, routines, supersets and a rest timer. Each set
  shows what you lifted last time.
- **Trends** over 7 and 30 days.

## Decisions worth explaining

### Offline first, not offline eventually

The food database is preprocessed by a small Python script into a compact file that ships
with the app. Search is instant and works in a basement gym. The network is only for
extras, like brand-name foods and barcodes.

### Your data stays yours

There's no account and no server. Everything lives in the phone's storage, with one-tap
export for backups and a gentle reminder if you haven't backed up in 30 days. Sharing
uses links that carry the data inside them, so even sharing doesn't upload anything.

### It works on the phone you actually have

iPhones don't support the browser's built-in barcode scanner, so the app checks what the
phone can do and switches to a bundled scanner when it has to. Scanning works the same on
both, offline included.

## What this means for your project

Not every app needs a backend. If yours doesn't, it can be faster, cheaper to run (often
free), and more private, and I'll tell you honestly when that's the case.
